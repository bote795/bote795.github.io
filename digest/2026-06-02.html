Brandon Foody, CEO of Mercor (a >$10B AI data/talent company doing $1B+ in revenue), argues that the application layer is structurally undefensible because "the model is the product" — Claude can clone Slack end-to-end within 12 months, gutting SaaS moats. He claims Mercor now spends more on tokens for internal agents than on employee salaries and predicts every Fortune 500 will look the same within 5 years. He also addressed the recent security incident, denied losing OpenAI as a customer, and made a strong call that at least one frontier lab (OpenAI or Anthropic) becomes a $10T+ company.

Key takeaways:

  • The Application Layer Has No Defensibility (Unless You Have Network Effects): Software wrappers on foundation models are dying because Claude Code can already replicate vertical SaaS features (medical, legal, finance) trivially. The only durable moats at the app layer are network effects (Salesforce ecosystem, Slack Connect, Cart). Without them, the company becomes worthless. Mindset shift: Before: "deep workflow product = moat." After: "deep workflow product = 12-month head start before the model swallows it."
  • Token Spend > Salary Spend Is the New Normal: Mercor now spends more on tokens for internal agents (AI project manager, interview agents, fraud detection, accounting) than on employee headcount. Foody predicts the average Fortune 500 enterprise will spend more on compute than on headcount within 5 years.
  • Jevons Paradox Is Eating Token Cost Reductions: Per-token costs are falling but total token consumption is rising faster — token costs are net rising for everyone. Don't bet on inference cost collapse to save you.
  • Forward Deployed > Go-to-Market: Pure sales motion gets eaten because savvy customers will just tell Claude to copy your SaaS. The durable wedge is post-sales forward deployed engineering — going deep with customers, training agents on their tacit knowledge, going the last mile. "Services are the new software" (Sequoia thesis) resonates.
  • Tacit Knowledge Is the Real Bottleneck, Not Data Cleanliness: Models can clean data themselves as reasoning improves (read 6 months of Slack, structure into CRM tables). The genuinely human contribution is the undocumented context that lives in employees' heads — that's the job of the next decade: codifying tacit knowledge and training agents on it.
